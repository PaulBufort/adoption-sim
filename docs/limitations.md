# Limitations

> Plain-language list of what this simulator cannot tell you. It exists from week 1
> and grows with the project. If a limitation ever disappears from this list, it is
> because the capability was actually built and validated — never because it was
> inconvenient.

## What this tool is

A demonstration and intuition instrument for *complex contagion* on *synthetic*
organizational networks. It shows how network structure, thresholds, and seeding
strategies interact **in the model**. It does not predict outcomes in any real
organization.

## Fundamental limitations (v0.1)

1. **All data is synthetic.** Every organization simulated here was generated by
   rules we wrote. No result is calibrated against any real rollout. A figure from
   this tool is an argument about *mechanisms*, never a forecast.
2. **Thresholds are unobservable.** Nobody knows the real distribution of adoption
   thresholds in any workforce. We chose a distribution (see decisions.md, D1–D2);
   plausible alternatives exist and would change quantitative outcomes.
3. **Credibility weights are stipulated, not measured.** "Close peer counts ~3× more
   than central comms" (D3) is an assumption encoding a qualitative literature claim,
   not an estimate.
4. **One behavior, one direction.** The model spreads a single adoption decision.
   No competing tools, no negative word-of-mouth (an unhappy adopter cannot
   discourage neighbors), no strategic or adversarial behavior.
5. **Time is uncalibrated.** A simulation step is "one round of social influence",
   not a day or a week. Curve *shapes* and *orderings* are meaningful; durations
   are not.
6. **People are simple.** Agents are threshold rules with three switches
   (ready/willing/able). Real people learn, negotiate, imitate selectively, and
   change their minds for reasons outside any network.
7. **The org generator is a caricature.** Real organizations have matrix reporting,
   contractors, turnover, reorgs, remote/office geography. Ours has departments,
   teams, roles, and tenure, full stop.
8. **Tenure does nothing behaviorally** (D13). It only shapes who knows whom.
   Any claim like "veterans resist change" is *not* in this model.
9. **Willingness is a coin flip, frozen at t=0** (D5). The model cannot represent
   persuasion campaigns that change minds over time — only exposure dynamics.
10. **Results depend on the random seed.** We report distributions over many seeds,
    but any single pretty curve is one draw. Beware of single-run screenshots.
11. **Imported real graphs are demonstrations only.** When we load e.g. the Enron
    e-mail graph, the edges are real but *everything else* (thresholds, weights,
    roles, willingness) is still synthetic. Such runs show the dynamics on a real
    *topology* — they say nothing about Enron, or about any real diffusion event.
12. **No claim of optimality.** "Cluster seeding beats broadcast in regime X" never
    means cluster seeding is the *best possible* strategy — only that it beats the
    compared alternatives under stated assumptions.

## Known technical limitations

- Synchronous updates can create mild wave artifacts (D7); the literature baseline
  shares this property.
- Pivot-node analysis (D12) is restricted to a screened candidate set for cost
  reasons; a true exhaustive knockout is quadratic and not attempted.
- Network visualizations sample large graphs (>2,000 nodes) for legibility; the
  picture is an impression, not the data.
- The Streamlit demo runs single-core with small populations and few replicates;
  it is a pedagogical toy on top of the engine, not the experiment pipeline.
