\title{Disperse to ignite, concentrate to endure:\\
a decay-driven crossover in modular complex contagions}
\titlerunning{A decay-driven crossover in modular complex contagions}

\begin{abstract}
Should a costly new practice be seeded into a few dense teams, or scattered
across the organization? We study this on synthetic two-layer organizations
of dense small teams, using fractional-threshold contagion with
heterogeneous thresholds, an innovator atom, and credibility-weighted
exposure, under a \emph{paired} protocol: every strategy runs on the same 50
organizations with identical agent draws. Without decay, dispersed seeding
dominates wherever a
cascade can ignite: $+39.7$ percentage points of terminal adoption over
cluster seeding at the headline setting (95\% CI $[35.4, 43.9]$), winning 14
of 40 cells of a threshold$\times$budget map (up to $+43$\,pp; equivalent
within $\pm2$\,pp in 23; cluster wins none). Under
reinforcement-dependent decay the advantage shrinks
and \emph{reverses} between the tested relapse rates: dispersed cascades stop
growing, while clustered adoption stays near 30\% almost regardless of decay
strength. The sign of the contrast replicates on a real modular topology
(email-Eu-core, synthetic behavioral attributes). All data are synthetic
and bit-for-bit reproducible from one master seed.
\keywords{complex contagion, threshold models, seeding strategies,
organizational networks, agent-based simulation}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and related work}

Threshold models~\cite{granovetter1978} make costly adoption require
reinforcement from multiple credible sources. Cascades on random graphs obey a vulnerability
boundary~\cite{watts2002}; \emph{complex contagions} need wide bridges of
overlapping ties, which long-range shortcuts destroy~\cite{centola2007,centola2010}.
Network-intervention practice~\cite{valente2012} distilled this into
\emph{seed dense clusters, never scatter}---evidenced largely in populations
not already embedded in cohesive groups. Adjacent results
strain it: modularity aids cascades only up to an
optimum~\cite{nematzadeh2014}; dispersed neighbor seeding beats clustered
seeding in threshold models~\cite{ou2022}; seed placement is its own
measurement problem~\cite{guilbeault2021,tanase2026}; random ties match or
beat clustered ties unless interest decays
fast~\cite{wan2025,sassine2024}---a structural reach--reinforcement
trade-off. We test its \emph{seeding-strategy} analogue at fixed topology and
ask \emph{when decay reverses the ordering}. Real organizations are
modular---nearly everyone already sits inside a dense small team---so what
does cluster seeding buy when every seed is already surrounded by a cluster?
We answer with a transparent mechanistic agent-based model under a paired,
pre-declared protocol (decision log ships with the code).\looseness=-1

\section{Model and paired protocol}
\label{sec:model}

A generator produces two-layer organizations: a formal hierarchy (CEO, 8
departments, 249 line teams of mean size 8, $N{=}2000$) and an informal
influence network---dense within teams ($p{=}0.9$), locally concentrated
between ring-adjacent ``sister'' teams, sparse across departments (silo
$0.85$)---on which adoption propagates. Agent $i$ holds threshold
$\theta_i \sim \mathrm{Beta}(\mu\kappa,(1-\mu)\kappa)$, $\mu{=}0.30$,
$\kappa{=}20$, and is an \emph{innovator} ($\theta_i{=}0$) with probability
$0.025$. Exposure is the credibility-weighted share of contacts whose
adoption is visible,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{share}_i(t) = \frac{\sum_{j\in N(i)} v_j\, w_{ij}\, a_j(t{-}1) + b(t)}
                           {\sum_{j\in N(i)} w_{ij} + b(t)},
\label{eq:share}
\end{equation}
with credibility $w$ (close collaborator $1.0>$ manager $0.7>$ distant peer
$0.6>$ comms $0.3$), source visibility $v_j$ (default 1), and $b(t)$ a
transient comms term during a broadcast. Adoption requires
\emph{ready} ($\mathrm{share}_i\ge\theta_i$ and $\mathrm{share}_i>0$),
\emph{willing} (Bernoulli $0.85$), and \emph{able} ($1.0$ here); updates are
synchronous. Decay (off unless stated): an adopter relapses with probability
$\rho$ per step while $\mathrm{share}_i<r\,\theta_i$, where $r$ sets how much
reinforcement retention requires. We report \emph{terminal adoption} (adopted
share among active agents at the fixed point; 98--100\% of runs converge)
and, secondarily, \emph{cumulative reach} (ever-adopted share). Four seeded
strategies share a 5\% budget (100 seeds): \emph{random}, \emph{champions}
(top informal degree), \emph{cluster} (whole teams), \emph{one-per-team}
(round-robin coverage); \emph{broadcast} is a zero-seed awareness
baseline---fixed heuristics, not influence-maximization
optima~\cite{kempe2003,ou2022}.\looseness=-1

\emph{Paired protocol.} Every condition runs on the \emph{same} 50 fresh
organizations with \emph{identical} threshold\slash willingness\slash ability
draws (common random numbers), so strategy contrasts are paired
differences---between-organization variance (9--16\,pp) cancels. Two
confirmatory families (regime map, decay grid) were pre-declared and
Holm-corrected for both difference and TOST-equivalence against a
$\pm2$\,pp practical band; each cell gets a three-way verdict: \emph{win},
\emph{equivalent} (within $\pm2$\,pp, for the tested cells and model), or
\emph{uncertain}. Seeds were frozen before execution; all results come from
a single successful full-pipeline execution ($6\,350$
simulations).\looseness=-1

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\textbf{Without decay, dispersion dominates the ignition regime.} At the
headline setting, random seeding reaches $72.0\pm9.2$\% terminal adoption
(mean$\pm$sd) against $32.4\pm11.0$\% for cluster seeding: a paired
difference of $\mathbf{+39.7}$\,pp, 95\% CI $[35.4, 43.9]$. Degree targeting
adds ${\sim}3.9$\,pp (descriptive, outside the confirmatory families), and
coverage with one seed per team yields no clear difference from random
($-1.4$\,pp, CI $[-5.0, +2.2]$)---random already lands seeds in $82$ of 249
line teams (${\sim}205$ end at majority) vs.\ $13$ (${\sim}91$) for
cluster. Across the map (Fig.~\ref{fig:crossover}a), dispersion wins 14 of
40 cells---the diagonal ignition band, $+2.8$ to $+42.6$\,pp ($[39.1,
46.0]$ at the boxed headline cell, an independent paired sample at the same
parameters)---the strategies are equivalent within $\pm2$\,pp (TOST) in 23
cells (saturation at low $\bar\theta$, starvation at high $\bar\theta$/low
budget), 3 stay uncertain, and \textbf{cluster seeding wins nowhere}.
Broadcast converts $5.5\pm3.4$\% ($0.0$\% in all 12 runs without
innovators; independent panel).\looseness=-1

\textbf{Decay drives a crossover.} With relapse active
(Fig.~\ref{fig:crossover}b,c), the dispersion advantage falls monotonically
in $\rho$ and flips sign \emph{between the tested values}. When retention
requires full reinforcement ($r{=}1$): $+8.4$\,pp $[4.2, 12.7]$ at
$\rho{=}0.10$, $\mathbf{-5.3}$\,pp $[-9.0, -1.5]$ at $\rho{=}0.25$,
$-11.4$\,pp $[-14.8, -8.0]$ at $\rho{=}0.40$---the crossover lies between
$\rho{=}0.10$ and $0.25$. At the weaker coupling $r{=}0.5$ it comes later:
$+13.9$\,pp $[9.6, 18.3]$ at $\rho{=}0.10$, parity still uncertain at
$\rho{=}0.25$ ($-0.3$\,pp $[-4.1, +3.5]$), reversal at $\rho{=}0.40$
($-7.7$\,pp $[-11.5, -3.9]$). The mechanism is asymmetric: clustered
adoption barely moves ($32.4\%$ at $\rho{=}0$ to $29.3\%$ at the harshest
setting) while dispersed adoption collapses ($72.0\%$ to $17.9\%$).
Cumulative reach shows \emph{why}: at ($r{=}1$, $\rho{=}0.25$) random's
ever-adopted share is only $29.0$\% (vs.\ $72$\% without decay)---relapse
throttles the dispersed cascade \emph{while it grows}; retention ratios
(descriptive) are $0.99$ (cluster) vs.\ $0.86$ (random). Concentration does
not become a high performer under decay; it \emph{endures} near 30\% while
dispersion loses everything.\looseness=-1

\textbf{Robustness and a real topology.} The no-decay contrast keeps its
sign across one-at-a-time organization variants ($N\in\{500, 2000, 8000\}$,
team size $\{5, 12\}$, silo $\{0.5, 0.7, 0.95\}$): paired $\Delta$ from
$+14.7$ to $+48.3$\,pp, all CIs excluding zero. On the real email-Eu-core
topology (986 nodes, 42 ground-truth departments; \emph{all behavioral
attributes synthetic}) the contrast keeps its sign: $+13.5$\,pp
$[1.0, 26.0]$ over 50 paired attribute draws. Adoption there is a bimodal
ignition lottery: random ignites in 62\% of draws, cluster in 42\%. This is
a \emph{sign replication
on a real modular topology}, not an empirical validation of the
mechanism.\looseness=-1

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Once everyone already lives in a dense team, guaranteed local
reinforcement---cluster seeding's defining asset---is no longer scarce, and
scattering buys more ignition surface per seed. Decay changes not who wins
ignition but \emph{whether ignition survives}: dispersed adoption needs
continuing growth that relapse throttles; clustered adoption sits on local
critical mass that relapse barely touches. The prescription is conditional
and asymmetric: \emph{disperse to ignite} when
adoption reinforces itself; \emph{concentrate to endure} when reinforcement
erodes quickly. Global visibility rescales
thresholds exactly ($\theta/v$, unit-tested), so low visibility slides the
organization across Fig.~\ref{fig:crossover}a and can change which strategy
wins (correcting an earlier corollary). A pre-registered local-ignition
predictor failed its validation gate (AUC $0.64<0.80$): most
dispersed-seeding ignition is \emph{not explained by the local predictor};
two-level modeling is future work. All of this holds within a synthetic, uncalibrated
model family---thresholds unobserved, time abstract, decay parameters
stipulated on a grid; a 19-entry limitations file ships with the
code.\looseness=-1

\emph{Reproducibility.} Every modeling decision (D1--D22) is logged with
alternatives and status; analysis families pre-declared, seeds frozen, one
successful full-pipeline execution. CI fails if any published number
changes; every statistic is machine-extracted and audited against the
manuscript. Repository: AGPL-3.0, public at publication.\looseness=-1
